Lessons for beginners

1. What is a synthetic index?

A synthetic index is a computer-simulated market, open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Its price moves according to controlled randomness, without depending on news or companies.

You do not own anything: you simply predict whether the price will go up or down over a chosen duration.

Key pointNo weekends and no surprise news: you can practise at any time.

2. Reading a candlestick

Each candlestick summarises the price over a period. The body runs from the opening price to the closing price; the wicks show the highest and lowest prices reached.

A green candle closed higher than it opened; a red candle closed lower.

Key pointA candle tells you what just happened, not what will happen next.

3. Trend, support and resistance

An uptrend makes higher and higher highs; a downtrend makes lower and lower lows. With no clear direction, the market is “ranging”.

Support is a level where the price has often bounced up; resistance is a level where it has often been pushed back down.

Key pointThese landmarks organise your analysis, but no level holds every time.

4. The MA, EMA and BB indicators

The moving average (MA) smooths the price to show its overall direction. The EMA does the same but reacts faster to the latest prices.

Bollinger Bands (BB) surround the price: wide bands signal a busy market, tight bands a calm one.

Key pointAn indicator describes the past: use it to understand, never as a certainty.

5. Managing your money

Set a budget you accept to lose and only risk 1 to 2% of that budget per trade.

Keep the same stake after a loss: doubling up to “win it back” empties an account very quickly.

Key pointWhat matters is not one trade, but consistency over hundreds of trades.

6. Trader psychology

Fear makes you close too early, greed makes you stake too much: both lead to losses.

Take a break after three losses in a row and write down your trades to understand your mistakes.

Key pointPractising on a demo account first lets you learn without pressure.

Trading glossary

Candlestick
Representation of the price over a period: open, close, high and low.
Wick
Thin line above or below a candle showing the highest and lowest prices reached.
Trend
Overall direction of the price: up, down or sideways (range).
Support
Price level where falls have often stopped.
Resistance
Price level where rises have often stopped.
Volatility
Strength and speed of price movements.
Stake
Amount committed to a trade.
Payout rate
Percentage earned on top of the stake if the prediction is right.
Duration
Time after which the trade ends and the result is known.
Buy (up)
Predicting the price will be higher at the end of the duration.
Sell (down)
Predicting the price will be lower at the end of the duration.
Demo account
Practice account with virtual money, with no risk at all.
Moving average (MA)
Line averaging recent prices to smooth the chart.
EMA
Moving average that gives more weight to the most recent prices.
Bollinger Bands (BB)
Two bands around the price that widen when the market gets busy.
Risk management
Rules to limit losses: small stakes, a budget set in advance, breaks.

Mistakes to avoid

1
Staking too muchDo not exceed 1 to 2% of your capital per trade.
2
Trying to “win it back”After a loss, keep the same stake and take a break.
3
Trading without a planDecide before you start: market, duration, stake and maximum loss for the day.
4
Changing method all the timeTest a method long enough on demo before deciding whether it suits you.
5
Skipping practiceTest every idea on a demo account before using real money.
6
Trading tired or upsetEmotions distort decisions: come back later.
